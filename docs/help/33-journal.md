title: Journal
parent: Weft help
summary: A private diary in your personal space, one page per day, that only you can read or search.
---
**Journal** is a private diary: one page for each day, for what happened, what you thought, or what you want to remember. Nobody else can read it.

### Writing

Open **Journal** on Home. It opens on today. Type in the page and press **Save page** (or Ctrl+Enter, Cmd+Enter on a Mac). The page is kept as plain text, up to 20,000 characters.

To write about another day, use **Previous day** and **Next day**, or pick a date in the date box; **Today** brings you back. You cannot write a page for a day that has not come yet.

Change a page and press **Save** again. **Delete page** removes it, and the words cannot be brought back. A page with nothing in it is not saved: delete it instead.

If you have Weft open in two places, a page you save in one shows up in the other straight away (unless you are in the middle of typing on that page there).

### Finding a page

On the left, your pages are listed newest first, each with its first line. Type in **Search your journal** to list only the pages with those words; clear the box to see them all again.

The **Your journal** widget on Home shows your latest pages, or a button to write today's. Click a page to open it. It is not on by default; add it with **Customize**. Keep in mind that anyone looking at your screen can read what the widget shows.

### Who can see what

Only you. Your pages live in your personal space, and the database lets nobody else read, search, change or delete them: not other people, not channel owners, not Weft admins, and not someone you add to your personal space.

Your pages appear in **your own** Weft search (under **Your journal**, marked "Only you"), and Weft (the assistant) can use them when you ask it something in **your Weft conversation** at the top of the chat. It never uses them when you ask in a channel, a direct message or your personal space, because other people could read the answer there, and it cannot change or delete them. Keep in mind that, as with anything you ask Weft, the parts of your pages it uses are sent to the model that writes the answer. AI apps you connect to Weft never find your journal. When you delete a page, Weft keeps a note that a page for that day was deleted, but not what it said.

People who run the Weft server can still reach the database and its backups directly, as with everything else in Weft.
