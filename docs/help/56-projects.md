A channel can hold **projects**. A Scouts channel might have a "Scouting for Food" project and a "Summer camp" project. Each project has its own chat, its own instructions for Weft, and a few pinned documents. Your personal space works the same way.

### Finding your way

- Under a channel's name in the chat, **General** is the channel's own chat and each project has a button next to it. Choose one to see that project's chat.
- In Documents, Wiki, Tasks and the other apps, a channel's things are grouped by project, with the rest underneath.
- To make something in a project, choose the project in the box next to **New** (for example "#scouts › Scouting for Food").
- To put something you already have into a project, open it and choose the project under **Move to…**. You stay its author when you move it.

### The calendar

- When you add an event, choose the project in the **Space** box (for example "#scouts › Scouting for Food").
- To put an existing event in a project, open it and choose under **Project**. A repeating event moves into the project all together, and every repeat made later stays in it.
- **Project** in the calendar's side bar shows just one project: its events and the tasks in its task lists. Milestones, shifts, bookings and time off belong to the whole channel, so they step aside while a project is chosen.
- **Download as a file** can download just one project's events.
- When you ask **@Weft** in a project's chat to add something to the calendar, the event goes into that project.

### Who can do what

- Everyone in the channel sees all its projects. Projects have no members of their own: if something should be private, make a separate channel for it.
- Anyone in the channel can chat in a project.
- **Owners** of the channel make, rename, archive and delete projects, write their instructions, and pin documents.
- **Archive** a project when it is over: its chat stays readable but takes no new messages. **Delete** removes its chat; its documents and events stay in the channel with no project.

### Instructions for Weft

When you ask **@Weft** in a project's chat, it follows these, in order:

1. **Weft's own rules.** It answers only from what you can see, links where each answer came from, and never makes things up. Nothing below can change that.
2. **The channel's rules**, set by the channel's owners under **Rules** (with General chosen).
3. **The project's instructions**, set by the owners under **Project settings**.
4. **Your own rules**, from your account menu under **Your rules for Weft**. They only change how answers to *you* look (length, format, tone), never the facts, and only when you ask.

A line under the channel's name says which rules Weft is following there. Earlier versions of the channel's rules and a project's instructions are kept.

Instructions tell Weft *how* to answer; they are not something it can quote. Put facts (dates, places, who does what) in a document and **pin** it to the project: open the document and choose **Pin for Weft**. Weft then always reads pinned documents first when it answers in that project. A project can pin up to 10.

### AI agents and channels

Owners can turn off **Allow AI agents to read this channel** under **Rules**. AI agents are people's own assistants connected to Weft, such as Claude through the connector, or a personal agent like OpenClaw or Hermes. When it is off, they see nothing in the channel, and what members write there never reaches them. Weft's own assistant is not affected.

AI agents can read what their person can see, but they can only make changes in that person's **Personal** space. To share something an agent made, move it into a channel or project yourself.
