**Glossary** keeps the words a group takes for granted in one place: abbreviations ("FOH"), nicknames for places ("Gate B", "P04"), and the jargon a newcomer hears on day one and is too shy to ask about. A glossary belongs to a space (a channel, or your personal space), and only the people in that space can see it.

### Making a glossary and adding words

Open **Glossary** on Home. Choose the space at the top and press **New glossary**. Type a name over "Glossary", such as "Festival words". A channel can have more than one, but one is usually enough.

Press **Add a term** and fill in:

- **Term**: the word or abbreviation, up to 80 characters, such as `FOH`.
- **Meaning**: what it means, in plain words, up to 2000 characters. Say where or when it matters if that helps: "Front of house: everything the audience sees, from the gate to the bar."

Each term can be in a glossary only once, whatever its capitals: if "FOH" is there, "foh" is refused, and Weft says so above the list. Change the existing one instead.

Press **Edit** or **Delete** on a term to change or remove it. Rename, move, share or delete a whole glossary the same way as a document. Moving it to another channel moves all its terms with it.

### Looking a word up

- Terms are listed A to Z under letter headings. Click a letter at the top to jump to it.
- Type in the find box to show only terms whose word or meaning contains all the words you typed.
- Ask Weft: "what does FOH mean?" Weft reads the glossaries you can see when it answers, so it can explain your group's words, and it can use them when they come up in other questions. In a channel, it uses only that channel's glossaries.
- The search box at the top finds glossaries by the words in their terms and meanings.
- The **New terms** widget on Home lists the newest terms in glossaries you can see. It is not on by default: add it with **Customize**.

### Who can see what

Everyone in the glossary's space can read it; nobody else can, not in search and not in Weft's answers. Editors and owners add, change and delete terms. Viewers can read and look words up, but not change anything. Weft can read glossaries to answer questions, but it cannot add, change or delete terms.
