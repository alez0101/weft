### Schemas

- `weft`: what the API exposes. Users, spaces, members, messages, items (documents, wiki pages, whiteboards, tables, task lists, polls, forms, link lists, address books, decision logs, Q and A boards, checklists, expense lists, inventories, goal sets, retros, glossaries, meetings, files), events, table columns and rows, tasks, board columns, poll choices, votes and open/closed state, form questions, answers and open/closed state, links, contacts, glossary terms (`glossary_terms`), decisions (`decisions`), checklist steps, runs and ticks (`checklist_steps`, `checklist_runs`, `checklist_run_steps`), expenses and their shares (`expenses`, `expense_shares`, and the `expense_balances` view), inventory things and the record of check outs and check ins (`inventory_things`, `inventory_moves`), goals and their key results (`goals`, `key_results`), retros and their notes (`retros`, `retro_notes`), Q and A (`questions`, `answers`, `answer_votes`), announcements, meetings and their action item links, people's profiles (`profiles`), RSVPs (answers to events), things to book and their bookings (`resources`, `bookings`), shifts and who is on them (`shifts`, `shift_signups`), who is away when (`time_off`), file bytes (`file_blobs`), embeddings, audit, read markers and each person's Home layout (`user_prefs`, readable only by them).
- `weft_private`: helpers, provider settings and keys, personal keys (`personal_providers`, encrypted, readable only by the assistant service), run logs, assistant proposals, embedding state, the admin log, and sign-in (password hashes, link and session hashes). Not exposed.

### Spaces: the permission unit

Everything belongs to one **space**: a channel, a direct message, a personal space or a Weft conversation. You see something only if you are a member of its space. Roles in a space: **viewer** (read and chat), **editor** (also create and change content), **owner** (also manage the space and its people). Sharing an item into another channel posts a link only; it grants nothing.

Two kinds of channel are kept by Weft itself: the help channel (`everyone`: every account is a viewer) and this admin channel (`admins_only`: exactly the active admins). Neither can be renamed or deleted from the app, and the admin channel's members cannot be changed by hand.

### The one rule

`weft_private.member_role(space)` returns your role in a space, or nothing. Every policy builds on it (`is_member`, `can_edit`, `is_owner`). It reads the token's user, applies the optional `scope_space` claim (so a channel-scoped token sees only that channel), and returns nothing for a disabled account.

### Database roles

- `authenticator`: PostgREST's login; can only switch roles.
- `weft_anon`: nothing at all.
- `weft_user`: every person.
- `weft_assistant`: the assistant acting for a person. Reads what they can, posts as Weft, changes and creates events. Never deletes, never edits documents.
- Service logins, each allowed one narrow job: `weft_auth` (sign-in), `weft_notify` (memberships for live notices), `weft_assist` (assistant settings and embeddings), `weft_collab` (switches to `weft_user` only), `weft_mcp` (the connector's apps, approvals and tokens, all hashed; it reads Weft only through the API as the person).

### Details that matter

- Column grants keep authorship honest: `author_id` and `created_by` cannot be written; they default to the token's user. Other people's last sign-in is not readable.
- Moving an item needs edit rights in both spaces. Thread replies and wiki parents must be in the same space.
- Audit triggers keep the old row of every change and delete, with who did it, readable only by members of that space.
- A table's rows also become a text summary on the table, so search and the assistant can read them.
- A task list is an item (kind `tasklist`); its tasks (`weft.tasks`) reach the space through the list, like table rows: members read, editors and owners write, the assistant role only reads. `created_by` and `done_at` are set by the database; `list_id` cannot be changed, so a task never jumps lists. A task can be assigned only to a member of the list's space, and moving a list to another space unassigns anyone not in it. The list's `body_text` is a summary of its tasks (who, when, done), for search and the assistant.
- A board (the Boards app) is a task list seen as columns, not an item of its own: its cards are the list's tasks. The open columns are `weft.board_columns` (name, position; at most 10 per list), which reach the space through the list like tasks: members read, editors and owners add, rename, reorder and delete, viewers read only, the assistant role only reads; `list_id` cannot be changed. Every task list gets "To do" and "Doing" when it is made (028 gave existing lists the same). Done is built in: it is the tasks with `done = true`. `tasks.column_id` says which open column a card is in (null, or a deleted column, means the first); the database refuses a column from another list, and an assignee who is only a viewer still may change `done` and nothing else, the column included. The list's summary names the column of open cards ("in Doing"), so search and the assistant know a card's stage.
- A poll is an item (kind `poll`) whose title is the question; its choices (`weft.poll_choices`), votes (`weft.poll_votes`) and open/closed row (`weft.polls`, made with the item) reach the space through it. Members read all of it, including who voted for what. **Every member votes, viewers included** (like chat, voting is taking part, not editing); a vote is one per person per poll (primary key), its `voter_id` is set by the database, its choice must belong to the same poll, and only the voter changes or withdraws it. Editors and owners change choices and close or reopen; who closed it and when are set by the database. A choice with votes cannot be renamed or removed, and a closed poll's choices and votes do not change. Moving a poll keeps its votes. The poll's `body_text` is the vote count per choice (no names), for search and the assistant. The assistant role only reads.
- Search is one function, `weft.search`, run as the caller: prefix matching on every word, titles ranked above text, snippets with the matches marked, over messages, items, events and channel names, with text indexes behind it. Only letters and digits from the search reach the query.
- Published wiki pages are `managed` and read-only in the app.
- A form is an item (kind `form`) whose title is its name; its questions (`weft.form_questions`: short, long or one choice, optionally required), answers (`weft.form_responses`, one row per person per form, `{question id: text}`) and open/closed row (`weft.forms`, made with the item) reach the space through it. **Every member answers, viewers included**; the respondent is set by the database, and only they change or withdraw their answer. The database checks each answer against the form's questions (required answered, a choice is one of the choices, lengths, no answers to other forms' questions). **Answers are not public in the space**: a person reads their own, editors and owners read everyone's (the same applies to the assistant acting for them). So the form's `body_text` (questions and the answer count), its live notices (ids only) and its audit rows (an answer's old row without its answers) never carry answer text. Editors and owners change questions and close or reopen; once anyone has answered, existing questions cannot be reworded, retyped or removed (new ones can be added), and a closed form's questions and answers do not change. Moving a form keeps its answers; people outside the new space, including the respondents, then cannot read them. Copying answers into a Sheets table is done in the browser, as the editor, into a new table in the same space. The assistant role only reads.
- A link list is an item (kind `linklist`) whose title is the list's name; its links (`weft.links`: address, title, note, up to 10 tags) reach the space through it, like tasks: members read, editors and owners add, change and delete, viewers read only, the assistant role only reads. Only `http` and `https` addresses are stored (checked in the table), so a stored link cannot run script; Weft never fetches the pages. Tags are trimmed, lowercased and de-duplicated by the database. `created_by` is set by the database and `list_id` cannot be changed, so a link never jumps lists. Moving a list moves its links. The list's `body_text` holds every link (title, address, tags, note), for search and the assistant.
- An address book is an item (kind `addressbook`) whose title is the book's name; its contacts (`weft.contacts`: name, organisation, phone, email, notes) reach the space through it, like links: members read, viewers included; editors and owners add, change and delete; the assistant role only reads. `book_id` is not updatable (a contact never jumps between books; moving the book moves them all), `created_by` defaults to the caller and is never granted. Email must be one plain address (no spaces, `?`, `&`, `/`, brackets or quotes; the domain only letters, digits, dots and hyphens) and phone only digits, spaces and `+ - ( ) . / x`, so the app's `mailto:` and `tel:` links cannot carry anything else. `body_text` holds every contact (name, organisation, phone, email, notes) for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (with the book id) and audited under the book's space.
- An announcement (`weft.announcements`: title, text, end time) belongs to a channel directly, like an event, and is not an item: members read, viewers included; editors and owners post, change, end early and delete; the assistant role only reads. Only channels have announcements (the database refuses personal spaces, direct messages and the assistant space, but only after the caller is known to be allowed to post there, so the refusal tells an outsider nothing). A new notice ends in the future and at most a year ahead (a week if no end is given); ending early is a change of `expires_at`. `created_by` is set by the database and `space_id` cannot be changed, so a notice never moves channel; deleting the channel deletes its notices. Ended notices are kept (the app lists them as ended); the chat strip and Home widget filter on `expires_at > now()`. Changes go through the shared notice and audit triggers. Announcements are not in search, meaning search or the assistant's context.
- A meeting is an item (kind `meeting`) whose title is the meeting's name; its row in `weft.meetings` (made with the item: time, agenda, notes, decisions, each up to 20,000 characters, an optional event and an optional task list) reaches the space through it: members read, viewers included; editors and owners change; nobody inserts or deletes meeting rows directly; the assistant role only reads. The event and the task list must be in the meeting's own space (one answer for "elsewhere" and "does not exist"). Action items are ordinary tasks (`weft.tasks`) in that list; `weft.meeting_actions` (task, meeting, `created_by` set by the database, never updated) records which tasks belong to which meeting, and a task can only be linked to a meeting in its list's space. `weft.add_meeting_action` makes the task and the link in one transaction, **as the caller** (security invoker), so the same policies decide; the assistant role cannot run it. The Tasks rules apply to action items unchanged (the assignee may tick theirs off, even as a viewer). When a meeting, its task list or its event moves to another space, every link that would cross spaces is cleared by triggers (the tasks stay in their list); deleting a meeting keeps its tasks. The meeting's `body_text` holds the time, event title, agenda, notes, decisions and action items, for search and the assistant. Changes to meetings and removed action items are audited; notices `meetings` and `meeting_actions` carry ids only.
- An RSVP (`weft.rsvps`: event, person, answer `going`, `maybe` or `no`; primary key event and person) reaches its space through the event (`weft_private.event_space`), like a vote through its poll; no copy of the space is kept. Members of the event's space read every answer with names, viewers included; every member answers, viewers too (like poll votes), but only for themselves: `user_id` defaults to the caller and is never granted, and `event_id` cannot be updated. The assistant role only reads. Once an event has ended (`ends_at < now()`) nobody adds, changes or clears an answer; deletes made by the database itself (inside another trigger: an event or person deleted, a move, someone leaving) always pass. When an event moves to another space, answers from people who are not members there are deleted; when someone leaves or is removed from a space, their answers on its events are deleted, so a name never shows where its owner cannot see. Changes and clears are audited under the event's id; the notice `rsvps` carries the event, the person and the space, ids only. Answers are not in search or `body_text`; the assistant role can read the table.
- A thing to book (`weft.resources`: name, note) belongs to a channel directly, like an announcement, and is not an item; its bookings (`weft.bookings`: thing, start, end, note, `booked_by`) carry the thing's space, copied by the database on insert (never granted), so the shared notice and audit triggers apply. Members read both, with names, viewers included; editors and owners add and rename things and book; a booking is changed or cancelled by whoever made it (while still an editor) or by an owner of the channel; only owners delete a thing, which deletes its bookings. The assistant role only reads. Only channels have things to book (refused after the caller is known to be allowed, like announcements). **No double booking** is an exclusion constraint (`btree_gist`, `resource_id` with `=` and `tstzrange(starts_at, ends_at, '[)')` with `&&`), so it holds under concurrent inserts; a trigger checks first to give a plain answer (`23P01`), but only for someone allowed to book the thing, so an outsider gets the same refusal whether a slot is taken, free or the thing does not exist. A new booking ends in the future and lasts at most 31 days; an ended booking cannot be changed or cancelled (deletes made by the database, inside another trigger, pass). `resources.space_id` and `bookings.resource_id` are not updatable, so neither ever moves. A person who leaves a channel keeps their bookings there until an owner cancels them. Bookings show in the Calendar (read-only) and are not in search, `body_text` or the assistant's context.
- A shift (`weft.shifts`: title, start, end, places 1 to 200, note) belongs to a channel directly, like a thing to book, and is not an item; a sign-up (`weft.shift_signups`: shift, person, unique per shift and person) carries the shift's space, copied by the database on insert (never granted), and `user_id` defaults to the caller and is never granted, so the shared notice and audit triggers apply and nobody signs up someone else. Members read both, with names, viewers included. **Every member signs up, viewers included** (like an RSVP), only themselves; you give back your own place, and editors and owners of the channel can take anyone off; editors and owners add, change and delete shifts. The assistant role only reads. Only channels have shifts (refused after the caller is known to be allowed). **Never over-filled**: the sign-up trigger locks the shift row (`for update`) before counting its places, so concurrent claims on one shift wait for each other; a shift's places cannot drop below the people on it (`23514`, with the number). Full, ended, already-on and no-such-shift answers are given only to members of the shift's channel; an outsider gets the same row-level security refusal in every case. A new shift ends in the future and lasts at most 7 days; once a shift has ended its sign-ups and the shift itself are fixed (it can still be deleted; deletes made by the database, inside another trigger, pass). `shifts.space_id` is not updatable and sign-ups are never updated, so nothing moves between channels. Someone who leaves or is removed from a channel loses their sign-ups there (audited under that channel). Shifts are not in search, `body_text`, the Calendar or the assistant's context.
- Time off (`weft.time_off`: person `user_id`, `starts_on`, `ends_on` dates with both days included, note up to 200 characters, `created_by` set by the database) belongs to a channel directly, like a shift, and is not an item. Members read every row with names, viewers included. Every member, viewers too, adds, changes and removes their own (`user_id = uid()` and `is_member`, like an RSVP); editors and owners (`can_edit`) do so for anyone in the channel. The person must be a member of the channel and only channels keep time off; both checks answer only someone allowed to write the row, so an outsider gets the same refusal whatever was sent. An entry lasts at most 366 days. `space_id` and `user_id` are granted on insert only and `created_by` never, so nothing moves or is forged. Leaving or being removed from a channel deletes your time off there (audited under that channel); deleting the channel deletes it all. Shared notice (`time_off`, ids only) and audit triggers; the assistant role only reads. Time off shows in the Calendar (all-day, read-only) and the Home widget **Out this week**, and is not in search, `body_text` or the assistant's context.
- A profile (`weft.profiles`: role, up to 12 skills, how to reach, when available; one row per person, `user_id` the primary key) belongs to a person, not a space, and is not an item. **You read a profile if its owner is a member of a space you are a member of**: the read policy checks `weft_private.is_member` on each of the owner's memberships, so the channel-scope claim and disabled accounts apply as everywhere (a #festival-scoped token sees only #festival members' profiles), and leaving the last shared space hides it again. Because every account is in the help channel, in practice every active account sees every profile; the scope claim is what narrows it. **Only the person writes their own profile**: `user_id` defaults to the caller and is never granted, and the write policies also need an unscoped token and an active account (`is_member` of their own personal space, `weft_private.personal_space`). Owners, admins and the assistant cannot change it; the assistant role reads. Skills are trimmed, lowercased and de-duplicated by the database before the limit of 12 (each at most 40 characters) is checked. A change is announced once per space the person is in (notice `profiles`, the person's id only), so only co-members hear of it; old rows are audited under the person's personal space, so only they read the history. Deleting the account deletes the profile. Profiles are not in search or `body_text`.
- A decision log is an item (kind `decisionlog`) whose title is the log's name; its decisions (`weft.decisions`: what was decided, the context, who decided as free text, the date, and optionally the earlier decision it replaces) reach the space through it, like contacts: members read, viewers included; editors and owners add, change and delete; the assistant role only reads. `log_id` is not updatable, `created_by` defaults to the caller and is never granted. A decision can replace only one in the same log (a composite foreign key on `(log_id, replaces_id)`, so a link never points into another space), never itself, never in a loop (refused by the guard trigger), and each decision is replaced by at most one other (a unique index); deleting a decision un-links what replaced it. `body_text` holds every decision (date, title, who, what it replaces or is replaced by, context) for search and the assistant. Changes are announced with the log id and audited under the log's space.
- A Q and A board is an item (kind `qa`) whose title is the board's name; its questions (`weft.questions`: question, details, accepted answer), answers (`weft.answers`) and upvotes (`weft.answer_votes`, primary key answer and voter) reach the space through the board, question and answer: members read all of it, with names, viewers included; editors and owners ask and answer; the assistant role only reads. **You change only your own question or answer** (the update policy needs edit rights, and the guard trigger refuses rewording someone else's, owners included); editors and owners delete any question or answer; deleting a question deletes its answers and their upvotes. **Every member upvotes, viewers included** (like poll votes), once per answer, only as themselves (`voter_id` defaults to the caller and is never granted), never their own answer (refused with a reason only to members; an outsider gets the row-level security refusal); an upvote is cast or withdrawn, never updated. The **accepted answer** (`questions.accepted_id`) is set by the person who asked or an owner of the space (the guard trigger), is empty on insert, and must answer the same question (a composite foreign key on `(id, accepted_id)` to `answers (question_id, id)`, `on delete set null`, so deleting it clears the mark; that database change passes the guard). `board_id`, `question_id` and `answer_id` are not updatable and `created_by` is never granted. Moving a board moves everything with it and keeps upvotes. `body_text` holds each question (newest first, up to 200) with who asked, the details and up to 5 answers, the accepted one first, then by upvotes, each with its upvote count and author, for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (notice `qa` with the board id, ids only) and audited under the board's space (an upvote's audit row uses the answer id).
- A checklist is an item (kind `checklist`) whose title is the template's name; its steps (`weft.checklist_steps`: label, position; at most 100), its runs (`weft.checklist_runs`: optional name, who started it and when, finished, who finished it and when) and each run's own copy of the steps (`weft.checklist_run_steps`: label, position, done, who ticked and when) reach the space through it: members read all of it, viewers included; editors and owners change the steps and start, tick, finish, reopen and delete runs; the assistant role only reads. **Starting a run copies the steps** in the after-insert trigger, so a run never changes when the template does; a checklist with no steps cannot be run (a plain answer, `23514`, given only to editors). A run's steps are never inserted or deleted by a person (no grants, no policy) and only `done` is granted; `done_by`/`done_at`, `started_by`/`started_at` and `finished_by`/`finished_at` are set by the guard trigger, never granted. A finished run's ticks are refused (`23514`) until it is reopened. `checklist_id` and `run_id` are not updatable, so nothing moves except with the whole checklist. `body_text` holds the steps and the 20 newest runs (who started, how many done, who finished, what was not done) for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (notice `checklists`, with the checklist id) and audited under the checklist's space; the copy made when a run starts is not audited or announced row by row.
- An expense list is an item (kind `expenselist`) whose title is the list's name; its expenses (`weft.expenses`: title, amount in whole cents, who paid, date, split `even` or `amounts`) and each expense's shares (`weft.expense_shares`: person and share in cents, primary key expense and person) reach the space through it: members read all of it, viewers included; editors and owners add, change and delete; the assistant role only reads. **The shares always add up to the amount**: a deferred constraint trigger checks every expense when the transaction commits (at least one share, the sum equal to the amount, and for an even split no two shares more than a cent apart), so an expense and its shares are written together, normally through `weft.save_expense` (security invoker, so row-level security, the column grants and the guard all apply; it works out an even split itself, the odd cents to the first people listed). **Who paid and every person in a split must be members of the list's space** when they are added (the guard trigger, `23514`, an answer given only to editors); someone who later leaves stays in the record and the expense can still be changed. `list_id`, `expense_id` and `user_id` are not updatable and `created_by` is never granted. `weft.expense_balances` (security invoker) sums paid, share and net per person per list from what the caller can read. There are no payments: paying back is recorded as another expense. `body_text` holds the total, each person's balance, a settle-up list (largest debts first) and up to 200 expenses, for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (notice `expenses` with the list id, ids only) and audited under the list's space (a share's audit row uses the expense id).
- An inventory is an item (kind `inventory`) whose title is the inventory's name; its things (`weft.inventory_things`: name, where it is kept, a note, who has it now `holder_id` and since when `held_since`) and the record of check outs and check ins (`weft.inventory_moves`: thing, `out` or `in`, whose, done by, when) reach the space through it: members read all of it, viewers included; editors and owners add, change and delete things and check out to or in from anyone in the space; the assistant role only reads. **Every member, viewers included, may borrow**: a second update policy lets a member update a thing that is in or that they have, ending in or with themselves, and the guard trigger refuses any other column change by someone who may not edit (`42501`). **Whoever has a thing must be a member of the space** when it is checked out to them (the guard, `23514`, an answer given only to members); someone who later leaves keeps it in the record until an editor checks it in. `inventory_id` is not updatable; `created_by` and `held_since` are never granted (`held_since` is set by the guard when the holder changes). The record is written only by the after trigger when `holder_id` changes (a hand-over writes an `in` then an `out`, timed with `clock_timestamp()` so they keep their order); nobody has insert, update or delete on it. Moving an inventory moves its things and record; a holder outside the new space stays the holder. `body_text` holds how many things and how many are out, then each thing by where it is kept (up to 300) with who has it and since when, for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (notice `inventory` with the inventory id, ids only) and audited under the inventory's space.
- A goal set is an item (kind `goalset`) whose title is the set's name; its goals (`weft.goals`: title, note, optional due date, position) and their key results (`weft.key_results`: title, start, target and current value, optional unit, position) reach the space through it (a key result through its goal): members read, viewers included; editors and owners add, change, delete and update values by hand; the assistant role only reads. A key result's `progress` is a generated column, (current − start) / (target − start) kept between 0 and 1, so a target below the start counts down and nobody can write a progress that does not match the numbers; the target must differ from the start and every number is below 10^12 in size. The guard stamps `value_updated_by` and `value_updated_at` (never granted) with the caller whenever the current value changes. `set_id` and `goal_id` are not updatable, `created_by` defaults to the caller and is never granted. A goal's progress is the average of its key results and the set's the average of its goals with key results, worked out by the browser from the stored `progress`. `body_text` lists each goal with its percentage, due date and note, then each key result as current of target (from start) and percentage, for search and the assistant. Changes are announced (notice `goals`, with the set id) and audited under the set's space; deleting a goal deletes its key results, and moving the set moves everything in it.
- A retro is an item (kind `retro`) whose title names it; `weft.retros` (made with the item: optional period from and to, and the task list its actions go to, which must be in the retro's space) and its notes (`weft.retro_notes`: column `went_well`, `to_improve` or `action`, text up to 1000 characters, an anonymous flag) reach the space through it. Members read and add notes, viewers included (taking part, like poll votes); you change the text of and delete only your own note; editors and owners set the period and list, delete any note and turn an action into a task with `weft.retro_action_to_task` (security invoker, so the Tasks rules apply; the task must be in a list in the retro's space). Anonymous notes: the author is kept in `created_by`, which is not granted for reading to any request role (select, filter, order and embedding by it are refused), `author_id` is set by the database and empty for an anonymous note, the audit log stores the old row without `created_by` and, for an anonymous note, no actor; `weft.my_retro_notes(retro)` (security definer) returns only the caller's own note ids. Column, retro and anonymity are fixed once posted. body_text lists the period and the notes by column, actions with their task's state, and never names anyone. A retro moved to another space drops its task list and task links unless they are in the new space, audited and announced under the old space; a task list moved away is dropped from retros elsewhere. The assistant role only reads.
- A glossary is an item (kind `glossary`) whose title is its name; its terms (`weft.glossary_terms`: term up to 80 characters on one line, meaning up to 2000) reach the space through it, like contacts: members read, viewers included; editors and owners add, change and delete; the assistant role only reads. A term appears once per glossary whatever its case (a unique index on `lower(term)`; the guard answers "already in this glossary" only once the caller may write there, so an outsider cannot probe which terms a glossary they cannot read holds). The database trims the term and squeezes its spaces; `glossary_id` is not updatable (moving the glossary moves its terms) and `created_by` defaults to the caller and is never granted. `body_text` holds one `Term: meaning` line per term, A to Z, so search, meaning search and the assistant find it; the assistant's prompt labels it as the group's own words. Changes are announced (notice `glossary` with the glossary id) and audited under the glossary's space.
- A file is an item whose `body_text` is its extracted text; its bytes are in `file_blobs`, readable only through the item (same rule) and never changed in place. The assistant role reads the text, never the bytes. The collab server refuses to open files, tables, task lists, polls, forms, link lists, meetings, Q and A boards, checklists, expense lists and inventories as documents.
