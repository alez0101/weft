### Schemas

- `weft`: what the API exposes. Users, spaces, members, messages, items (documents, wiki pages, whiteboards, tables, task lists, polls, forms, link lists, meetings, files), events, table columns and rows, tasks, board columns, poll choices, votes and open/closed state, form questions, answers and open/closed state, links, announcements, meetings and their action item links, file bytes (`file_blobs`), embeddings, audit, read markers and each person's Home layout (`user_prefs`, readable only by them).
- `weft_private`: helpers, provider settings and keys, personal keys (`personal_providers`, encrypted, readable only by the assistant service), run logs, assistant proposals, embedding state, the admin log, and sign-in (password hashes, link and session hashes). Not exposed.

### Spaces: the permission unit

Everything belongs to one **space**: a channel, a direct message, a personal space or a Weft conversation. You see something only if you are a member of its space. Roles in a space: **viewer** (read and chat), **editor** (also create and change content), **owner** (also manage the space and its people). Sharing an item into another channel posts a link only; it grants nothing.

Two kinds of channel are kept by Weft itself: the help channel (`everyone`: every account is a viewer) and this admin channel (`admins_only`: exactly the active admins). Neither can be renamed or deleted from the app, and the admin channel's members cannot be changed by hand.

### The one rule

`weft_private.member_role(space)` returns your role in a space, or nothing. Every policy builds on it (`is_member`, `can_edit`, `is_owner`). It reads the token's user, applies the optional `scope_space` claim (so a channel-scoped token sees only that channel), and returns nothing for a disabled account.

### Database roles

- `authenticator`: PostgREST's login; can only switch roles.
- `weft_anon`: nothing at all.
- `weft_user`: every person.
- `weft_assistant`: the assistant acting for a person. Reads what they can, posts as Weft, changes and creates events. Never deletes, never edits documents.
- Service logins, each allowed one narrow job: `weft_auth` (sign-in), `weft_notify` (memberships for live notices), `weft_assist` (assistant settings and embeddings), `weft_collab` (switches to `weft_user` only), `weft_mcp` (the connector's apps, approvals and tokens, all hashed; it reads Weft only through the API as the person).

### Details that matter

- Column grants keep authorship honest: `author_id` and `created_by` cannot be written; they default to the token's user. Other people's last sign-in is not readable.
- Moving an item needs edit rights in both spaces. Thread replies and wiki parents must be in the same space.
- Audit triggers keep the old row of every change and delete, with who did it, readable only by members of that space.
- A table's rows also become a text summary on the table, so search and the assistant can read them.
- A task list is an item (kind `tasklist`); its tasks (`weft.tasks`) reach the space through the list, like table rows: members read, editors and owners write, the assistant role only reads. `created_by` and `done_at` are set by the database; `list_id` cannot be changed, so a task never jumps lists. A task can be assigned only to a member of the list's space, and moving a list to another space unassigns anyone not in it. The list's `body_text` is a summary of its tasks (who, when, done), for search and the assistant.
- A board (the Boards app) is a task list seen as columns, not an item of its own: its cards are the list's tasks. The open columns are `weft.board_columns` (name, position; at most 10 per list), which reach the space through the list like tasks: members read, editors and owners add, rename, reorder and delete, viewers read only, the assistant role only reads; `list_id` cannot be changed. Every task list gets "To do" and "Doing" when it is made (028 gave existing lists the same). Done is built in: it is the tasks with `done = true`. `tasks.column_id` says which open column a card is in (null, or a deleted column, means the first); the database refuses a column from another list, and an assignee who is only a viewer still may change `done` and nothing else, the column included. The list's summary names the column of open cards ("in Doing"), so search and the assistant know a card's stage.
- A poll is an item (kind `poll`) whose title is the question; its choices (`weft.poll_choices`), votes (`weft.poll_votes`) and open/closed row (`weft.polls`, made with the item) reach the space through it. Members read all of it, including who voted for what. **Every member votes, viewers included** (like chat, voting is taking part, not editing); a vote is one per person per poll (primary key), its `voter_id` is set by the database, its choice must belong to the same poll, and only the voter changes or withdraws it. Editors and owners change choices and close or reopen; who closed it and when are set by the database. A choice with votes cannot be renamed or removed, and a closed poll's choices and votes do not change. Moving a poll keeps its votes. The poll's `body_text` is the vote count per choice (no names), for search and the assistant. The assistant role only reads.
- Search is one function, `weft.search`, run as the caller: prefix matching on every word, titles ranked above text, snippets with the matches marked, over messages, items, events and channel names, with text indexes behind it. Only letters and digits from the search reach the query.
- Published wiki pages are `managed` and read-only in the app.
- A form is an item (kind `form`) whose title is its name; its questions (`weft.form_questions`: short, long or one choice, optionally required), answers (`weft.form_responses`, one row per person per form, `{question id: text}`) and open/closed row (`weft.forms`, made with the item) reach the space through it. **Every member answers, viewers included**; the respondent is set by the database, and only they change or withdraw their answer. The database checks each answer against the form's questions (required answered, a choice is one of the choices, lengths, no answers to other forms' questions). **Answers are not public in the space**: a person reads their own, editors and owners read everyone's (the same applies to the assistant acting for them). So the form's `body_text` (questions and the answer count), its live notices (ids only) and its audit rows (an answer's old row without its answers) never carry answer text. Editors and owners change questions and close or reopen; once anyone has answered, existing questions cannot be reworded, retyped or removed (new ones can be added), and a closed form's questions and answers do not change. Moving a form keeps its answers; people outside the new space, including the respondents, then cannot read them. Copying answers into a Sheets table is done in the browser, as the editor, into a new table in the same space. The assistant role only reads.
- A link list is an item (kind `linklist`) whose title is the list's name; its links (`weft.links`: address, title, note, up to 10 tags) reach the space through it, like tasks: members read, editors and owners add, change and delete, viewers read only, the assistant role only reads. Only `http` and `https` addresses are stored (checked in the table), so a stored link cannot run script; Weft never fetches the pages. Tags are trimmed, lowercased and de-duplicated by the database. `created_by` is set by the database and `list_id` cannot be changed, so a link never jumps lists. Moving a list moves its links. The list's `body_text` holds every link (title, address, tags, note), for search and the assistant.
- An announcement (`weft.announcements`: title, text, end time) belongs to a channel directly, like an event, and is not an item: members read, viewers included; editors and owners post, change, end early and delete; the assistant role only reads. Only channels have announcements (the database refuses personal spaces, direct messages and the assistant space, but only after the caller is known to be allowed to post there, so the refusal tells an outsider nothing). A new notice ends in the future and at most a year ahead (a week if no end is given); ending early is a change of `expires_at`. `created_by` is set by the database and `space_id` cannot be changed, so a notice never moves channel; deleting the channel deletes its notices. Ended notices are kept (the app lists them as ended); the chat strip and Home widget filter on `expires_at > now()`. Changes go through the shared notice and audit triggers. Announcements are not in search, meaning search or the assistant's context.
- A meeting is an item (kind `meeting`) whose title is the meeting's name; its row in `weft.meetings` (made with the item: time, agenda, notes, decisions, each up to 20,000 characters, an optional event and an optional task list) reaches the space through it: members read, viewers included; editors and owners change; nobody inserts or deletes meeting rows directly; the assistant role only reads. The event and the task list must be in the meeting's own space (one answer for "elsewhere" and "does not exist"). Action items are ordinary tasks (`weft.tasks`) in that list; `weft.meeting_actions` (task, meeting, `created_by` set by the database, never updated) records which tasks belong to which meeting, and a task can only be linked to a meeting in its list's space. `weft.add_meeting_action` makes the task and the link in one transaction, **as the caller** (security invoker), so the same policies decide; the assistant role cannot run it. The Tasks rules apply to action items unchanged (the assignee may tick theirs off, even as a viewer). When a meeting, its task list or its event moves to another space, every link that would cross spaces is cleared by triggers (the tasks stay in their list); deleting a meeting keeps its tasks. The meeting's `body_text` holds the time, event title, agenda, notes, decisions and action items, for search and the assistant. Changes to meetings and removed action items are audited; notices `meetings` and `meeting_actions` carry ids only.
- A file is an item whose `body_text` is its extracted text; its bytes are in `file_blobs`, readable only through the item (same rule) and never changed in place. The assistant role reads the text, never the bytes. The collab server refuses to open files, tables, task lists, polls, forms, link lists and meetings as documents.

### Migrations

`db/migrations/001` to `029` rebuild the database from nothing, in order: roles, schema, access rules, triggers, chat reads, notices, service logins, assistant, collab, proposals, assistant events, everyone spaces, whiteboards, tables, semantic search, people, wikis, accounts, files and widgets, personal keys, search, tasks, polls, forms, the connector for AI apps, links, announcements, boards, meetings.
